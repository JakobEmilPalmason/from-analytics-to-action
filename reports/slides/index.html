1Project briefSpeaker 1 · 1:30

Why arthouse

Publikum sells audience insights for film and TV. Their three pillars are data, story, zeitgeist.

We narrowed the brief to arthouse because that is where the data alone cannot answer the question. Artistic ambition lives next to commercial fragility, and Publikum's clients have to make positioning calls under that tension.

Arthouse is a small share of European production but a large share of the conversation festivals and distributors actually have. Mis-positioning here is costly.

8.4% of films, most of the auteur conversation.
A small slice carries the brand weight for distributors, festival buyers, and the press.

The methods catch different canonical filmmakers. The rule finds Audiard and de Oliveira through specialty distributors and festival keywords. The LLM finds Dreyer and Sciamma through name recognition. Each method on its own would have missed half of what we wanted, so we kept the union.

6Recommendations & limitsSpeaker 6 · 1:30

What Publikum can do with this

1.
Position deliberately divisive films using the productive lexicon. A festival arthouse film framed around essay or surrealism reads as intentionally polarizing. The audience comes prepared to argue.
2.
Do not market transgressive films as controversial. The audience that hates them will not convert. Reframe via theme — psychological depth, social inquiry — rather than content.
3.
Weight love-share, not just polarization, when picking comparable titles for a new project. Two films with the same polarization can mean opposite things to a buyer.
Honest limitations The 50-film hand-validation has a ±14pp confidence interval near the middle of the scale. The rule still flags Basic Instinct via specialty distributor metadata. The LLM still mislabels obscure old foreign films as avant-garde. Theatrical performance, distributor deals, and award histories are not in the data we received.
